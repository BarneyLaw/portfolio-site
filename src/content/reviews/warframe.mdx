I have been playing Warframe for about ten years now. Not continuously, nobody plays anything continuously for ten years, but I keep coming back, and after a decade I feel qualified to say this is one of the few live-service games that actually respects the player.

## Why it holds up

The core loop is movement, and the movement is genuinely excellent. Bullet jumping, sliding, aim gliding, the whole traversal system feels better than most games that treat movement as their entire selling point. Once it clicks you stop walking anywhere ever again. That feel is the thing that survives every meta shift, every reworked system, every new expansion. The moment-to-moment act of playing is just fun.

The free-to-play model is the other reason I keep recommending it. You can earn premium currency by trading with other players, which means a patient free player can access essentially the entire game without spending anything. There is no energy system, no artificial timer gating your fun, no pay-to-win wall in a game where the only competition is against the environment. Compared to almost every other F2P title, that is close to a miracle.

## The honest caveats

It is not perfect, and ten years lets you see the seams.

The new-player experience is rough. The game throws systems at you with almost no explanation, and the early hours are the weakest part. The early star chart is a slog, and a lot of the depth is hidden behind dozens of hours you have to push through mostly on faith.

The systems have also accreted over a decade. There are mechanics layered on mechanics, some of which contradict each other or exist because of history rather than design. A new player in 2026 is walking into ten years of accumulated complexity all at once.

## Verdict

Highly recommended, with the asterisk that you need to survive the opening hours and ideally have a friend or a wiki to explain the early confusion. Once you are past that, it is one of the most rewarding and generous games I have ever put time into. Ten years and I still open it. That is the whole review, really.
